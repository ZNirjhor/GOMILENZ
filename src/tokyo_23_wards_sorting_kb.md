# Tokyo 23 Wards Household Waste Sorting — Knowledge Base

Version: 1.0. Compiled 2026-11-08 from official ward publications, plus one Tokyo Metropolitan Government (TMG) presentation and one tracking blog where noted.
Scope: household (not business) waste in Tokyo's 23 special wards.
Language: English. Japanese terms are given in brackets so users can match signs and calendars.

## How to use this file (for the model)

- Answer ONLY from this file. If an item or ward rule is not covered, say so and point the user to their ward's official guide or sorting app.
- Every rule below has a confidence tag:
  - [HIGH] = confirmed on an official ward page or PDF from 2023 onward.
  - [MED] = official source is older than the latest change, or confirmed only by a secondary source.
  - [CHECK] = known to vary between wards and not verified for every ward.
- Rules change (for example, Nerima changed its plastic rules in October 2026). Always tell users to confirm with the sign at their collection point (集積所) or their ward's calendar.

## Part A. Rules shared by all 23 wards

A1. Bags. There is no paid, designated bag in the 23 wards. Use transparent or semi-transparent bags (or a lidded container where the ward allows it). Black or opaque bags may be left behind. [HIGH — e.g. Setagaya 2026 English guide; Chuo burnable-waste page]

A2. Timing. Put waste out on the morning of collection day, usually by 8:00 a.m. Some areas use other times. For example, parts of Setagaya near Sangenjaya and Shimokitazawa stations need burnable waste out by 7:00 a.m., and Nerima's bottles, cans and PET bottles go to the collection spot by 9:00 a.m. Do not put waste out the night before. [HIGH — Setagaya, Ota, Bunkyo, Shinagawa, Nerima guides]

A3. Main categories found in every ward:
- Burnable waste (可燃ごみ / 燃やすごみ): kitchen scraps, paper that can't be recycled, rubber and leather items, small amounts of wood, diapers. Plastic goes here only in wards that don't collect it as a resource (see Part B).
- Non-burnable waste (不燃ごみ / 燃やさないごみ): metal, glass, ceramics, small appliances about 30 cm or smaller. Usually collected twice a month.
- Resources (資源): paper (newspapers, magazines, cardboard, cartons), food and drink glass bottles, food and drink cans, PET bottles. Usually weekly.
- Plastic (プラスチック / 資源プラスチック): in most wards a separate resource category. See Part B.
- Oversized waste (粗大ごみ): items with any side over about 30 cm. You must apply in advance (phone or online) and pay, then attach a fee sticker. [HIGH — Setagaya, Itabashi, Shinagawa, Katsushika]

A4. PET bottles (ペットボトル). Only bottles with the PET mark that held drinks or certain seasonings. Remove the cap and label, rinse, and put out on PET or resource day. Bottles for cooking oil or non-food contents are NOT PET resources. [HIGH — Setagaya, Shinjuku]

A5. Liquids are never collected. Empty containers fully. Absorb small amounts of cooking oil in paper or cloth and put them in burnable waste. Some wards also collect used cooking oil at drop-off points. [HIGH — Setagaya; Itabashi item list]

A6. Sharp and broken items: wrap knives, broken glass and similar items in thick paper and mark the package キケン ("danger"). Put them in non-burnable waste. [HIGH — Setagaya; Itabashi]

A7. Never collected by any ward:
- Air conditioners, TVs, refrigerators and freezers, washing machines and dryers. These fall under the Home Appliance Recycling Law: return them to a retailer, or contact the 23-ward Home Appliance Recycling Center (kaden23rc.jp).
- Personal computers: the maker takes them back under the PC3R scheme, or use a courier service such as Renet Japan.
- Fire extinguishers, tires, car batteries, paint, oil and petroleum, chemicals, pianos, safes, building materials (bricks, tiles, drywall), and soil or rocks.
- Medical injection needles: return them to the pharmacy or clinic.
[HIGH — Setagaya 2026 English guide; Itabashi]

A8. Lithium-ion and rechargeable batteries, and devices with them built in (mobile batteries, heated tobacco devices, wireless earbuds, handheld fans, electric shavers), must NEVER go in plastic or burnable waste. They cause fires in collection trucks and plants. How each ward collects them varies (see Part C). A swollen battery must not be left at a collection point: take it to the ward's waste office. [HIGH — Ota plastic page 2026; Setagaya; Nerima]

A9. Dirty plastic. In every ward that collects plastic, plastic that still has food or other residue after a quick rinse goes in burnable waste. [HIGH — Ota, Itabashi, Edogawa, Toshima, Sumida]

## Part B. How each ward handles PLASTIC (the main difference between wards)

Terms used below:
- Container/packaging plastic (容器包装プラスチック, 容リプラ): packaging that held a product, usually marked with the プラ mark. Examples: food trays, cups, bags, shampoo bottles, bottle caps, labels, cushioning.
- Product plastic (製品プラスチック, 製品プラ): items made ENTIRELY of plastic that are not packaging. Examples: toothbrushes, plastic hangers, rulers, toys without batteries, storage containers, buckets, CDs. Usually only under about 30 cm.

Background: TMG reports that plastic collection grew from 12 wards (FY2019) to 22 of 23 wards (FY2026). [HIGH — TMG Bureau of Environment presentation, September 2026]

### Common plastic edge cases

- Bottle cap and label from a PET bottle: plastic resource in Type 1 and 2 wards; burnable in Setagaya. [HIGH]
- Plastic hanger with a metal hook: Nerima accepts it as plastic. Ota and other strict "plastic only" wards do not; remove the hook if possible, otherwise follow that ward's rule for mixed materials (often burnable). Edogawa takes hangers at drop-off boxes. [Nerima, Ota: HIGH; others: CHECK]
- Toys: OK only if all plastic with no batteries or electronics. Toys with batteries are non-burnable, and the battery must be removed. [HIGH — Ota, Shibuya]
- Plastic items over about 30 cm (clothes cases, large buckets): oversized waste, except Edogawa's drop-off boxes (under 50 cm) and monthly box events (under 80 cm). [HIGH]
- Natto tubs, mayonnaise bottles and other plastic that's hard to clean: rinse 2–3 times. If solid residue remains, it goes in burnable waste. A leftover food colour stain alone is OK. [HIGH — Ota]

## Part C. Other known differences (verified wards only)

C1. Spray cans and cassette gas cylinders:
- Shinjuku: collected with bottles, cans and PET bottles as a resource. They need not be fully used up. Do NOT puncture. [HIGH]
- Shibuya: collected as a resource. [HIGH — 2022 flyer]
- Setagaya: non-burnable waste. Must be completely empty, in a separate bag labelled スプレー缶. [HIGH]
- Shinagawa: non-burnable waste. [HIGH]
- All other wards: [CHECK]. Tell the user it varies by ward and to check their ward's guide.

C2. Rechargeable batteries and devices with built-in batteries:
- Setagaya: non-burnable waste. Discharge fully, tape the terminals, put in a separate bag marked デンチ. Button cells go to collection cans in shops. [HIGH]
- Nerima: non-burnable day, in a separate bag from other non-burnable waste. [HIGH]
- Shinagawa: non-burnable waste. [HIGH]
- Shinjuku: dry-cell batteries are collected with resources (bottles/cans). Small rechargeable batteries have their own instructions on the ward page. [HIGH for dry cells; rechargeable: CHECK]
- All wards: never in plastic or burnable waste (A8). Other wards: [CHECK].

C3. Lighters: Setagaya — completely empty, in a separate bag labelled ライター, non-burnable. Shinagawa — non-burnable. Nakano — see C4. Others: [CHECK].

C4. Nakano (中野) — other differences [HIGH — Nakano leaflet, July 2026, unless noted]:
- Non-burnable waste is called 陶器・ガラス・金属ごみ (ceramics, glass, metal), collected twice a month. It includes pots and pans, aluminium foil, wire hangers, metal jar lids, blades (wrapped), and disposable hand warmers. Unbroken fluorescent tubes go to drop-off points instead.
- Spray cans, gas cartridges and lighters: each in a separate bag labelled with its contents, out on the 陶器・ガラス・金属ごみ day. Do not puncture them.
- Rechargeable batteries and products with them built in (mobile batteries, e-cigarettes, handheld fans, wireless earbuds, electric toothbrushes): since April 2026 collected at the collection point on the 陶器・ガラス・金属ごみ day, in a separate bag labelled リチウムイオン電池, with the terminals taped. A swollen or damaged battery goes to the 清掃事務所 (sanitation office), never the collection point. Never put them in burnable or plastic waste. (The April 2025 English leaflet sends them to drop-off boxes only; the 2026 leaflet is newer.)
- Dry-cell and button batteries: battery collection boxes at public facilities (April 2025 English leaflet) [MED].
- Bottles, cans and PET bottles go to a separate bottle/can/PET collection point, not the usual one. Only COLOURLESS PET bottles are accepted; coloured PET bottles go in burnable waste. Food and drink bottles and cans only; other bottles and cans go to 陶器・ガラス・金属ごみ.
- Paper and old clothes are collected by neighbourhood groups (集団回収) at their own points, weekly by 8:00 a.m. Clothes only on days with no rain forecast. Paper from businesses is not accepted.
- Umbrellas: the 2026 leaflet lists umbrellas, including ones over 30 cm, under 陶器・ガラス・金属ごみ. The April 2025 English leaflet listed them as oversized. Follow the 2026 leaflet, but confidence is MED.
- Oversized: any side over 30 cm. Heavy hard items under 30 cm (dumbbells, bowling balls) also count as oversized. Book on 03-5715-2255 or online.

## Part E. Known gaps (be honest about these)

- This file covers each ward's PLASTIC rules plus shared rules. It does NOT include each ward's full A–Z item dictionary (五十音順 分別辞典), which runs to hundreds of items per ward.
- Collection DAYS vary by street address and are NOT in this file. Send users to their ward's calendar or sorting app.
- Kita, Chiyoda, Taito and Shinagawa plastic rows are [MED]: the start date comes from a secondary tracking blog and the 23-ward annual statistics, not from a 2025–2026 ward page I read directly.
- Katsushika's exact bagging rule for product plastic is unverified.
- Spray cans, batteries, lighters, clothes and fluorescent tubes are verified only for the wards named in Part C. Nakano (C4) is the only ward with a full ward-specific section so far.
